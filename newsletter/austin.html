Round 18 · Grand Prix · Circuit of the Americas

Antonelli wins Austin from pole, but the crown waits for Mexico

Kimi Antonelli did his part with a dominant win in Texas, but Russell's eighth and Hamilton's fourth kept the title math alive for one more week.

What happened

Antonelli turned pole into a clean getaway and was never seriously threatened. Behind him, Verstappen briefly lost second to Leclerc on the opening lap and took it back before the lap was out. Antonelli pitted at the end of lap 24, rejoined fourth, and worked his way back past Leclerc, who had briefly led after Red Bull's stop. A one-stop run on mediums then hards, plus a Virtual Safety Car for Valtteri Bottas's stopped Cadillac that barely touched his margin, brought him home 13 seconds clear.

The fight for the other podium places was livelier. Leclerc passed Verstappen late for second, and Verstappen, complaining of an "undriveable" car and front wing trouble, still found a way past Lando Norris for third after Red Bull told Isack Hadjar to let him through. Russell's afternoon went wrong on lap one: he ran wide, nudged Oscar Piastri off the road and picked up a five-second penalty that he served at his lap-22 stop, ending up eighth.

Why it matters

Two of three boxes ticked

Antonelli needed a win, Russell ninth or lower, and Hamilton fifth or lower. He got the win, but Russell salvaged eighth and Hamilton finished fourth, one place too high. Close enough that the celebration is merely postponed.

Ferrari's upgrade looked real

With a new floor and sidepods, Ferrari put both cars in the top four and outscored Mercedes by a point. Hamilton is now one point behind Russell in the fight for second.

Verstappen is podium-proof

Even with a car he called undriveable, Verstappen made it three straight podiums and pulled within six points of Russell. Second place in the championship is now a four-way scrap separated by nine points.

What it means · The title math

Lead now: 123 · Points left after Mexico: 100 · Needs to leave Mexico 100+ clear

Five races and 125 points remain. After Mexico City, 100 will be left, and Antonelli wins any tie on race wins, so he is champion if he leaves Mexico at least 100 points ahead. He can afford to lose up to 23 points to Russell and 24 to Hamilton. A top-nine finish guarantees the title. Even without points, only a win by Russell or Hamilton would push the decision on to Brazil.
