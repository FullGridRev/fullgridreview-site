Round 19 · Grand Prix · Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez

Antonelli is world champion. Verstappen wins the race.

Third place in Mexico City made Kimi Antonelli the youngest champion in Formula 1 history, on a chaotic afternoon Max Verstappen won by eight tenths from Lewis Hamilton.

Stewards were still to look at the Piastri–Lindblad contact after the race.

What happened

Hamilton led away from pole, but the opening laps belonged to the stewards. The two Ferraris went side by side and wide at Turn 4, Russell lost places off the line with a deployment problem and then tagged Isack Hadjar into a spin, and Leclerc came out of the first stint with a five-second penalty for gaining an advantage off track. Verstappen, starting on mediums while the cars around him ran softs, stayed out of trouble. When Leclerc served his penalty at his lap-27 stop, Verstappen pitted on the same lap and came out ahead of the pair of them.

Then the race came apart. Alonso and Stroll both retired in the first ten laps, and Valtteri Bottas crashed heavily at Turn 1 after a suspension failure, bringing out the red flag for barrier repairs (he was checked and released unhurt). The restart order was taken from a lap earlier, which put Hamilton third and Antonelli fourth. Antonelli then dived past a locking-up Leclerc at Turn 1 for the podium place he didn't even need. A late Safety Car for Arvid Lindblad's crash after contact with Oscar Piastri bunched the field once more, but Verstappen broke clear through the stadium section at the final restart and Hamilton never got close enough to try a move.

Why it matters

The youngest champion ever

Antonelli wins the title at 20 years and 68 days, more than three years younger than Sebastian Vettel was in 2010. He is Italy's first champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953, and the first product of the Mercedes junior programme to take the crown. His first words after the flag: "It's difficult to put into words what I'm feeling right now."

Hamilton has a grievance

Hamilton converted his first Ferrari pole into second place, but spent the first stint convinced he had been pushed wide by his own teammate and that the stewards' slow response handed Leclerc the lead. With Leclerc losing out to the penalty anyway, Ferrari got both cars into the top four. The intra-team temperature is the story to watch in Brazil.

The fight for second is wide open

Verstappen's fourth podium in a row, and his win, lifts him from fourth to second in the championship. Just 16 points now cover Verstappen, Hamilton, Russell and Leclerc, with four races left. Russell, who started second, drops from runner-up to fourth.
